Cat Lab / field notes

Observation 01

Domestic gravity.

Cats turn ordinary rooms into territories. A chair becomes a lookout, a warm rectangle becomes a destination, and a closed door becomes a research question.

This lab watches those small choices: where attention lands, how routes repeat, and which objects acquire meaning through use rather than design.

2
Observation 02

Whisker maps.

Movement is measured before the body commits. Whiskers test gaps, ears rotate toward a sound, and paws negotiate edges one careful contact at a time.

The result is a living map made from touch, memory and repeated safe passages.

3
Observation 03

The slow blink.

Not every signal needs movement. A softened gaze and slow blink can reduce tension without closing distance. Quiet communication often works by removing pressure.

For the observer, the useful lesson is restraint: notice first, interpret second, interfere last.

4
Observation 04

Return routes.

Favorite paths reveal preference over time. Repeated jumps, resting places and inspection points form a pattern that is more reliable than a single dramatic moment.

Cat Lab ends where it began: with ordinary space, watched carefully enough that repetition becomes data.

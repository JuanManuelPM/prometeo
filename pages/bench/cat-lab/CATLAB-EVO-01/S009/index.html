STUDY ONE / MOTION

The quiet approach

A cat often tests a route before committing its full weight. Small pauses, lowered shoulders and a changing tail position turn movement into a sequence of continuous corrections.

Watch the moment before a jump: orientation, compression, distance check, launch, landing and re-balance are easier to compare when recorded as separate transitions.

02
STUDY TWO / ATTENTION

Ears before eyes

A resting cat can look still while its ears scan different directions. One ear may track a hallway while the face remains aimed at a person.

Attention is distributed rather than binary. Note which feature changes first after a small sound: ear angle, eye direction, head position or whole-body posture.

03
STUDY THREE / SOUND

The low-frequency room

Purring is not a one-word message. It may occur during relaxed contact and can also occur in uncertain contexts, so posture, distance and freedom to leave matter.

The Cat Radio below is deliberately synthetic: a local oscillator, filter and modulation create an ambient pulse without recordings or network assets.

04
STUDY FOUR / REST

Sleep as geography

A resting place solves several problems at once: temperature, visibility, escape routes, softness and social distance. A favorite condition can matter more than a favorite object.

Useful notes separate observation from inference. Record location, time and room activity, then compare repeated choices before naming a pattern.
